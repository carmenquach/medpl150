Week Four: "Nuclear War: The Rising Risk, and How We Stop It" by W.J. Hennigan


This reflection is about W.J. Hennigan’s interactive New York Times article titled “Nuclear War: The Rising Risk, and How We Stop It”. This article discusses the important issue of nuclear conflict, and how global society seems to be dismissing the severity of these topics. Hennigan argues that the world has become scarily detached from the threat of nuclear conflict, when in reality, its chances of occurring are increasing – especially considering our current global/political climate. By associating nuclear weapons strictly to past wars, like the Cold Wars and World Wars, we as society have become desensitized by the impending danger and threat of mass violence. As mentioned in his article, Hennigan states the following: “Nuclear nations are building up their arsenals, speeding toward the next arms race. Is anyone paying attention?” Our current political climate has only been exponentially increasing in tension, with nations across the globe are seeing an escalation in the deployment of weaponry. We as society do not notice the severity of nuclear war, and Hennigan argues that we must stay alert and understand the threat at hand.

Nuclear weapons are often mentioned in historical contexts, making it seem as if it’s an “ancient relic”. However, this is far from the truth. In the past few years, countries like Russia have been threatening other countries with nuclear strikes. The United States have also played significant roles in these conflicts, with the Biden administration developing nuclear plans as a response. This situation only further deepened when the Russian President Vladimir Putin threatened nuclear war if NATO were to involve themselves in the Russian and Ukraine conflict. Threats are being thrown left and right globally, and yet the world has been so desensitized by this danger. Hennigan explains how “an entire generation came of age in a post-Cold War world, when the possibility of nuclear war was thought to be firmly behind us.” This conveys how most of modern society have yet to experience the true threats of nuclear warfare, therefore they do not realize how “real” this threat actually is.

To further elevate his central thesis, Hennigan utilizes interactive visual elements in the article. The moment you access the article, the main idea is boldly displayed in the center, with a flickering red-to-black gradient. The flickering of the background colors resemble a warning light, alerting the readers of the risk of nuclear conflict. The font choices are deliberately handpicked to get the main point across – the headings are fully uppercased, marked bold, and aligned to the center of the page. The font sizes for both the headings and the body text are very large, ensuring its legibility to readers. As you read through the article, a parallax scrolling effect is implemented to fully grasp the audiences’ attention and interest. The violence of the graphics in the article serves as a warning to readers, asserting the central idea of how dangerous nuclear conflict can be globally.

All in all, Hennigan’s work was meant to be a necessary call to action, mentioning how society has dismissed the threat of the conflict entirely. To prevent the unimaginable from occurring, we must collectively contemplate the reality of our political climate and the possibility of modern nuclear warfare.
